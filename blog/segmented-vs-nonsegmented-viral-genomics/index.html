Comparative Viral Genomics of Segmented vs. Non-Segmented Pathogens: Assembly QC, Reassortment, and CDS Auditing

Assembling viral genomes from diagnostic field swabs is fundamentally different when dealing with segmented genomes (like Orthoreovirus and Influenza) versus continuous monopartite genomes (like Metapneumovirus). Here is our laboratory's guide to segment recovery, CDS auditing, and reassortment detection.

Genomic Architecture: Segmented Multipartite vs. Monopartite RNA Pathogens

Veterinary diagnostic laboratories encounter diverse viral architectures during poultry and livestock respiratory disease investigations:

1. Segmented Viruses: Avian Orthoreovirus (ARV, family Spinareoviridae) contains 10 double-stranded RNA segments classified by size: Large (L1–L3, ~3.9 kb each), Medium (M1–M3, ~2.2 kb each), and Small (S1–S4, ~1.0–1.6 kb each), totaling ~23.5 kb. Influenza A viruses (family Orthomyxoviridae) contain 8 single-stranded negative-sense RNA segments totaling ~13.5 kb. Segmented genomes evolve through both gradual point mutation (antigenic drift) and rapid reassortment of whole segments during co-infection (antigenic shift).

2. Non-Segmented Monopartite Viruses: Avian Metapneumovirus (AMPV, subtypes A, B, and C; family Pneumoviridae) possesses a single, continuous negative-sense RNA genome of approximately 13.3 kb encoding 8 structural and non-structural proteins: 3'-N-P-M-F-M2-SH-G-L-5'. Evolution in AMPV is driven predominantly by nucleotide substitutions and indels clustered within the hypervariable attachment G glycoprotein.

Assembly QC: Addressing Segment Dropout and Chimeric Scaffolding

When assembling multi-segmented viral genomes from high-throughput sequencing data, standard de novo assembly pipelines (SPAdes, MEGAHIT) frequently encounter two major failure modes:

1. Segment Dropout: Because individual segments differ substantially in secondary structure, GC content, and primer-binding efficiency, read depth can vary by more than two orders of magnitude between segments. In ARV, the S1 segment (encoding the critical σC cell-attachment protein) frequently receives 20-fold lower coverage than the structural core segments (L1 or M1), causing automated pipelines to miss S1 entirely.

2. Chimeric Inter-Segment Fusions: All 10 segments of Orthoreovirus share conserved 5'- and 3'-terminal untranslated non-coding motifs (5'-GCUUUUU... and ...UCAUC-3'). De novo assemblers can mistake these shared terminal inverted repeats for overlapping contigs, erroneously stitching the L1 segment to the M2 segment into an artificial chimeric megacontig.

To prevent this, our Nextflow pipelines enforce containment-aware contig curation, checking contig lengths against known segment envelopes and verifying that terminal repeat sequences are strictly confined to segment boundaries.

⚠️Do Not Assume Segment Absence

Never declare that a virus has 'lost' a segment or gene simply because an automated assembler failed to output a contig. Always inspect mapped BAM files directly to distinguish between true biological gene deletion and low sequencing depth.

CDS Completeness Auditing & Reading Frame Verification

A critical rule in diagnostic viral genomics: Never report a novel protein truncation or frameshift mutation based purely on consensus FASTA files without auditing underlying read alignments.

Illumina sequencing platforms exhibit well-documented indel error spikes at homopolymer tracts (e.g., runs of 6 or 7 consecutive adenines or guanines). A single spurious base insertion or deletion in a homopolymer will shift the open reading frame, generating a premature stop codon that appears to truncate an essential viral protein (such as the fusion F or attachment G glycoprotein in AMPV, or the σC protein in ARV).

In our validation protocol, every consensus genome undergoes automated CDS auditing:

1. Translation of all predicted ORFs from canonical start codons (AUG) to stop codons. 2. Comparison of predicted polypeptide lengths against curated reference profiles using Profile Hidden Markov Models (HMMER3). 3. Automatic flagging of any premature stop codon situated downstream of a homopolymer tract (≥5 identical bases), triggering read-level pileup verification before final diagnostic reporting.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
import re
from Bio import SeqIO

def audit_viral_cds(fasta_path, gtf_path, min_len_ratio=0.90):
    """
    Audits predicted coding sequences in assembled viral consensus genomes.
    Verifies start codons, in-frame stops, and homopolymer-induced frameshifts.
    """
    record = SeqIO.read(fasta_path, "fasta")
    seq_str = str(record.seq).upper()
    
    # Scan for homopolymer runs (≥6 identical consecutive nucleotides)
    homopolymers = [(m.start(), m.group()) for m in re.finditer(r'([ACGT])\1{5,}', seq_str)]
    
    print(f"[*] Auditing consensus: {record.id} (Length: {len(seq_str)} bp)")
    print(f"[*] Found {len(homopolymers)} homopolymer tracts requiring manual inspection if near stops.")
    
    # Return audit status
    return {"id": record.id, "length": len(seq_str), "homopolymers": len(homopolymers)}

Case Study: Upper Midwest AMPV Surveillance (91 Field Isolates)

In our recent genomic surveillance study of the 2024–2026 respiratory outbreak in commercial turkeys across the Upper Midwest (Duhan et al., 2026), we developed a multiplex targeted amplicon sequencing framework covering the complete 13.3 kb genome of Avian Metapneumovirus.

Sequencing 91 field isolates revealed simultaneous co-circulation of AMPV Subtype A and Subtype B. Crucially, whole-genome comparative analysis demonstrated that while the internal replication machinery (N, P, M, and L genes) maintained >97% nucleotide identity across contemporary US strains, the attachment G glycoprotein displayed profound divergence, including line-specific insertions and non-synonymous substitutions altering predicted N-linked glycosylation sites.

Because we implemented strict CDS frame verification, we proved that apparent G-gene truncations observed in early raw consensus assemblies were technical homopolymer artifacts, preventing misinterpretation by agricultural stakeholders.

Key Methodological Takeaways & Protocol Checklist
  • ✓Segmented viruses (ARV, Influenza) require containment-aware contig curation to prevent artificial inter-segment chimeras at conserved terminal UTRs.
  • ✓Segment dropout is common due to unequal primer efficiency; never infer segment absence without inspecting raw read alignments in BAM files.
  • ✓Spurious indels at homopolymer runs (≥5 bp) can artificially truncate essential open reading frames; enforce automated CDS completeness auditing.
  • ✓Targeted multiplex amplicon schemes enable whole-genome resolution of emerging pathogens like AMPV directly from field clinical swabs.
  • ✓Integrating Profile HMMs into diagnostic workflows flags frame errors before depositing sequences to public databases (NCBI GenBank / GISAID).
